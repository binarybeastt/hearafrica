# Hackathon assessment

My assessment: this could become a competitive entry, but the current prototype is not enough to confidently call it a winner. Its strongest feature is situated spoken practice: learners must greet, ask, negotiate and repair misunderstandings with progressively less help. The map supplies context and visual appeal. The learning outcome must carry the pitch.

## Fit with the supplied categories

- **Access & Inclusion — strongest:** language education in Nigeria, if you demonstrate who lacks useful practice and how this helps them participate. Internet/API dependence and language quality are current limitations, so do not claim universal access or offline operation.
- **Open / Wildcard — credible:** a useful cultural and language-learning experience even without a jobs or finance use case.
- **Future of Work — possible extension:** local-language practice for customer-facing staff or people relocating for work. The current scenarios do not yet demonstrate hiring or workplace outcomes.
- **FinTech & Commerce — weak as currently built:** bargaining is a learning simulation. There is no actual payment, marketplace or financial tool.
- **Civic Tech — weak as currently built:** an interactive cultural map does not by itself solve a public infrastructure or governance problem.

## A focused pitch to test

“HearAfrica helps people moving to a new Nigerian community practise everyday conversations before they need them in real life.”

Choose one learner group first, such as NYSC members posted outside their home language region. That is a proposed target audience, not a validated customer segment. Interview a few members before presenting it as established demand.

## The demo I would enter

1. Show one learner need and one market scenario in roughly 15 seconds.
2. Use L1 to learn a respectful greeting and hear the trader answer.
3. Repeat with L2, completing the phrase rather than clicking a supplied answer.
4. Try L3 without displayed dialogue, recover with “please repeat”, then finish the negotiation.
5. Show independent task completion and ask the learner to repeat the skill in a slightly different situation.

Use one well-reviewed language for the judged demo. Have fluent speakers check phrases, tones, audio and feedback. Show a few real learner trials and report what happened; do not fabricate improvement percentages. Rapport and price are game variables, not evidence of learning by themselves.

Before a public demo, replace a shared browser API key with short-lived server-issued credentials and usage controls. For a robust competition presentation, prepare a clearly labelled recording in case venue connectivity fails. Do not label a scripted fallback as live AI.

The biggest improvements would be evidence of learning, reliable audio and native-speaker validation. Adding more map regions, languages or categories would be lower priority.
